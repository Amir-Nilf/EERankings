What does this measure?
Research output. Each area has a short list of top venues, and schools are compared on what their faculty publish there. Surveys and reputation aren't used.
How is the count calculated?
Each author on a paper gets the same share of credit. A school's total in an area is the sum of its faculty's credit there.
credit=1number of authors
The count is the geometric mean of those totals across the N selected areas. This favors schools that are strong in several areas over schools with one very large area.
count=(∏a(totala+1))1/N−1
Who counts as faculty?
There's no hand-checked faculty list for these fields, so faculty are inferred from OpenAlex. Someone counts if their latest paper in these venues lists a university and they were last or corresponding author on at least 3 papers in this discipline's venues. All of their papers count toward their current school, whatever department they're in. Some students and postdocs get through and some faculty get missed, so don't read too much into small differences.
Why these venues?
They're the flagship journals for each area, plus flagship conferences where those matter. Click an area's name to see its venues and turn them on or off individually.
Where do the method and data come from?
The scoring method follows CSRankings, created by Emery Berger for computer science. This site is independent and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by that project. Publication data comes from OpenAlex (Priem, Piwowar & Orr, 2022), released under CC0.
What is the Advisor Index?
A second ranking for choosing a grad school. You'll work with one or two advisors, so what matters is how many active, well-cited people a school has in your area. Click Advisor Index next to FAQ to turn it on.
  1. Influence. Each paper has a value that depends on its citations and is split equally among its authors. The log keeps one very highly cited paper from dominating.
    paper value=1+ln(1+citations)number of authors
    A person's influence is their percentage of that venue's total for that year, which makes old and new papers and small and large fields comparable.
  2. Recency. Influence is weighted so it halves every 3 years, counted back from the last selected year.
    weight=2−age/3
  3. Advisor strength. In each area, a person's influence is averaged over the selected venues and square-rooted, then added up across the school's faculty. This way two active advisors beat one person with the same combined output.
    strengtha=∑facultyinfluenceavenues selected
  4. Score. The final score combines the N selected areas, then gets scaled so the top school in view is 100. A weak area lowers a school's score but doesn't wipe it out.
    score=(1N∑astrengtha)2
The weights are a judgment call, not an established standard. Citations can be inflated, faculty are still inferred, and the index doesn't know about funding, mentoring, placement or student life, so use it for a shortlist and talk to current students.
